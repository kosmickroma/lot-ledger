# PIA Emails — Texas CAD Historical Ownership Data

Drafted 2026-05-14 for KK's parcel-history backlog. Each markdown file contains the recipient address, subject line, and full email body ready to copy/paste.

**Scope:** "all available digital years through 2020" — open-ended, lets each CAD respond with their max. Goal is to assemble ownership history sufficient to identify the last 3-4 owners per parcel (typically 30-40 years of data given ~10-year average turnover).

## Send order (whichever's easiest first)

| File | County | Recipient | Standard PIA window |
|---|---|---|---|
| `01_dcad_dallas.md` | Dallas (DCAD) | `openrecords@dcad.org` — single direct email | 10 business days |
| `02_tad_tarrant.md` | Tarrant (TAD) | Web form OR `info@tad.org` OR call (817) 284-0024 | 10 business days |
| `03_collin_cad.md` | Collin CAD | Web form OR `webmaster@collincad.org` OR call (469) 742-9200 | 10 business days |
| `04_denton_cad.md` | Denton CAD | Contact form OR `info@dentoncad.com` OR call (940) 566-0904 | 10 business days |

DCAD is the cleanest — they publish a direct PIA inbox. The other three you may have to use a contact form or follow up by phone if they don't respond within a few days.

## Before sending — quick browser check (~5 min)

Each county's public download page might have been updated since the research. Confirm what's already free so the PIA only asks for what's missing. If a county has more public years than expected, just narrow the requested year range:

- `https://www.dallascad.org/dataproducts.aspx` (confirmed 2021-2025 public)
- `https://www.tad.org/data-download/`
- `https://collincad.org/open-data-portal/` (routes to Texas.gov)
- `https://www.dentoncad.com/data-extracts/`

If TAD already publishes 2015+, narrow the PIA request to 2010-2014. Same for Collin/Denton — narrow as the public coverage allows.

## Expected outcomes

- Best case: $0 cost, files delivered as download links within 10 days
- Likely case: $50-200 per county for staff labor pulling archived files
- Worst case: $500+ or "we can't extract that format anymore" — at which point we evaluate Regrid or another commercial source
